**Line:** "I've been doing breathing exercises for my anxiety, and now my anxiety is doing them back, only better."

## The joke

The setup is the most ordinary wellness scene there is: someone with anxiety is told to breathe, and they comply. The turn is that the anxiety refuses to be the patient. Instead of sitting quietly while the exercise is performed on it, it picks up the routine and performs it back — and does it better. That is the everyday truth underneath the gag: coping strategies don't defeat an anxious mind, they get absorbed by it, copied, and turned into one more arena where you lose on points. Anyone who has ever been smug about a meditation streak and then anxious about the meditation streak knows the exact feeling.

Three things make it work technically. First, the anxiety becomes an agent. "My anxiety is doing them back" gives it hands and a smirk, which is funnier than any description of how it feels. Second, the final two words, "only better," are the button: the joke could end at "doing them back" and be merely clever, but "only better" raises the stakes by admitting your coping skill has been outclassed by the thing it was meant to fix. Third, nothing is explained — no clause tells the reader the anxiety got worse, the reader works that out and enjoys having done so.

An earlier version ended on "which is now mostly anxiety about inhaling wrong." That had a decent premise and a flat finish: the relative clause explained the punch instead of delivering it, "mostly" hedged the laugh, and "breathing exercises... inhaling" repeated the same root twice. Swapping the explanatory clause for a rival who acts, and closing on "only better," moved the surprise to the last two words and cut the scaffolding.
